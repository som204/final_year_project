Executive Summary

The academic year demonstrated a departmental operational net surplus, with adjustments for depreciation and amortization amounting to ₹30,000. This figure represents the departmental operational result prior to the allocation of central overheads, indicating a positive financial standing at the departmental level before broader institutional cost distributions.

Academic Performance

The institution's academic landscape is characterized by significant student enrollment across its various departments. The Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department leads with 2200 students, followed by Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) with 1600 students. The Information Technology (IT) department enrolls 1200 students, while Chemical Engineering and Automation and Instrumentation Engineering (AEIE) departments have 800 and 700 students, respectively. Specific student records indicate active enrollment at both undergraduate and graduate levels across these departments.

An analysis of academic performance for a cohort of 28 first-year students in the CSE department reveals a broad distribution of achievements. Cumulative Grade Point Averages (CGPAs) for this group ranged from 5.09 to an outstanding 9.9. Five students achieved a CGPA of 9.0 or higher, with Benjamin Wilson attaining the highest score of 9.9. Conversely, five students recorded CGPAs below 6.0. The average CGPA for this specific cohort was approximately 7.32, reflecting a generally robust academic standing.

Further supporting academic excellence, the institution awarded a total of 15,000 in tuition scholarships to five students. These scholarships were distributed across different departments and academic levels, including 2,000 to an undergraduate in CSE, 1,000 to an undergraduate in ECE, 5,000 to a graduate student in CSE, 3,000 to an undergraduate in Chemical Engineering, and 4,000 to a graduate student in AEIE. These awards underscore the institution's commitment to recognizing and fostering high academic achievement among its diverse student population.

Financial Overview

Revenue, expenses, and budget performance
University of Toronto

Financial Overview

The institution's financial performance for the period reflects a robust operational framework. Total operating revenue reached INR 1,015,000, primarily driven by Tuition & Fees contributing INR 425,000. Significant contributions also came from Government Grants & Contracts at INR 280,000 and Research Grants & Sponsored Programs amounting to INR 150,000. Further diversification of income streams was observed through Donations & Endowment Income of INR 90,000, Auxiliary Enterprises generating INR 45,000, and Other Income accounting for INR 15,000.

Operating expenses for the period totaled INR 1,000,000. The largest component was Salaries & Benefits, accounting for INR 540,000, underscoring the institution's investment in its human capital. Academic Program Expenses stood at INR 120,000, while Research Expenses were INR 110,000. Student Services & Scholarships required INR 70,000, and Facilities, Maintenance & Utilities amounted to INR 90,000. Administrative & General costs were INR 50,000, with Depreciation & Amortization at INR 30,000. This resulted in an operating surplus of INR 15,000. After accounting for non-operating items, including net investment gains of INR 10,000 and interest expense of INR 3,000, the net surplus before transfers was INR 22,000. Including transfers and capital grants of INR 8,000, the change in net position for the period was INR 30,000.

From a budgetary perspective, the institution planned for a total revenue of INR 1,000,000 against total expenses of INR 985,000, targeting a planned operating surplus of INR 15,000. Strategic allocations included a Capital Expenditure Budget of INR 75,000, supporting infrastructure development and asset acquisition. The Scholarship & Financial Aid Budget was set at INR 85,000, demonstrating a commitment to student support. Furthermore, INR 120,000 was allocated for internal Research Funding, fostering academic innovation, and a Contingency Reserve of INR 15,000 (1.5% of total revenue) was established for unforeseen circumstances.

An analysis of departmental financial performance reveals varying contributions to the institution's overall financial health. The Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department generated INR 150,000 in revenue against expenses of INR 120,000, yielding the highest departmental surplus of INR 30,000. The Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) department reported revenue of INR 110,000 and expenses of INR 92,000, resulting in a surplus of INR 18,000. The Information Technology (IT) department achieved a surplus of INR 15,000 from INR 90,000 in revenue and INR 75,000 in expenses. The Chemical Engineering department recorded INR 70,000 in revenue and INR 65,000 in expenses, leading to a surplus of INR 5,000, while the Applied Electronics and Instrumentation Engineering (AEIE) department generated INR 60,000 in revenue with expenses of INR 52,000, resulting in a surplus of INR 8,000.

Student Achievements & Placements

University of Toronto

Student Achievements

The academic year demonstrated significant progress in student achievements, particularly in academic excellence. A notable number of students achieved outstanding Cumulative Grade Point Averages (CGPA), reflecting their dedication and scholarly commitment. Benjamin Wilson from the CSE Department achieved an exceptional CGPA of 9.9, leading the cohort. Other high achievers include Noah Miller from IT with a CGPA of 9.7, Ethan Gonzalez from Chemical Engineering with 9.42, and Noah Lopez from IT with 9.41. Furthermore, Harper Miller (IT) secured a CGPA of 9.32, and Benjamin Martin (IT) achieved 9.06. Across various departments, students like James Smith (CSE) with 9.31, Alexander Jackson (CSE) with 9.25, and Amelia Miller (CSE) with 9.24 also showcased exemplary academic performance, with a total of 10 students achieving a CGPA of 9.0 or above.

Regarding financial support and recognition through scholarships, specific details on awarded scholarships and financial aid provided to students are not available within the provided context. The institution remains committed to supporting deserving students, and further information on such initiatives would typically be detailed in dedicated financial aid reports.

The career placement outcomes for the graduating cohort underscore the practical readiness and industry demand for our students. Several students secured highly competitive positions with leading national and international companies. Amelia Davis from the IT Department achieved a remarkable Cost to Company (CTC) of 28.8 LPA with Capgemini, while Harper Miller, also from IT, secured 26.3 LPA with IBM. Charlotte Martin from IT received an offer of 24.9 LPA from Accenture. In the Computer Science and Engineering department, Ethan Garcia secured 21.3 LPA with Amazon, and Ella Johnson achieved 21.4 LPA with Google. Other significant placements include Abigail Taylor (CSE) with 14.6 LPA at TCS, Evelyn Martinez (ECE) with 14.5 LPA at Texas Instruments, and Benjamin Martin (IT) with 13.0 LPA at Wipro. The placements spanned diverse sectors, with students joining companies such as Reliance (11.7 LPA for Liam Wilson, Chemical), Shell (13.0 LPA for William Martin, Chemical), ABB (11.5 LPA for Evelyn Smith, AEIE), and Samsung (9.1 LPA for Harper Jones, ECE), demonstrating the broad appeal of our graduates across various engineering disciplines.

Placements & Career Services

The academic year demonstrated a robust performance in placements, with a comprehensive analysis of the financial summaries for 62 students across various departments. The overall average Cost to Company (CTC) for these students stood at ₹7.34 Lakhs per annum, reflecting competitive compensation packages. Individual CTCs ranged from a minimum of ₹5.01 Lakhs to a maximum of ₹9.94 Lakhs per annum, indicating a wide spectrum of opportunities secured by the graduating cohort.

Within the Computer Science Engineering (CSE) department, data for 25 students revealed an average CTC of ₹7.21 Lakhs per annum. The salary packages for CSE graduates ranged from ₹5.53 Lakhs to ₹9.91 Lakhs per annum, showcasing strong demand for their skills and expertise across various industries. This performance underscores the quality of education and preparedness of CSE students for the professional landscape.

Similarly, the Information Technology (IT) department reported an impressive average CTC of ₹7.44 Lakhs per annum, based on the financial summaries of 37 students. Graduates from the IT department secured packages ranging from ₹5.01 Lakhs to ₹9.94 Lakhs per annum. These figures highlight the consistent success of IT students in securing lucrative positions, contributing significantly to the overall placement achievements of the institution.

Faculty Accomplishments

The institution maintains a robust faculty contingent across its departments, totaling 151 members. Among the detailed faculty records available, a strong academic foundation is evident, with 25 faculty members holding postgraduate or doctoral degrees. Specifically, 8 faculty members possess an M.Sc., 6 hold an M.Tech., and 5 have earned a Ph.D., demonstrating a significant commitment to advanced academic qualifications. Additionally, 6 faculty members hold a B.Tech. degree, contributing to the diverse educational background of the teaching staff.

The faculty body exhibits a broad spectrum of professional experience, ranging from 3 years to an impressive 22 years. This blend of seasoned expertise and emerging talent ensures a dynamic learning environment. For instance, in the Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department, experience levels among the listed faculty vary from 4 years to 20 years, with notable contributions from faculty members like Steven Lee and Wendy Lee, each with 20 years of experience. Similarly, the Information Technology (IT) department benefits from extensive experience, exemplified by Quentin Garcia with 22 years and Eva Brown with 18 years, alongside newer faculty such as Michael King, who brings 3 years of experience.
