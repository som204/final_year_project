Analysis of Student Progress

The academic year demonstrated significant progress in student outcomes, with a notable increase in the number of students achieving their academic goals. According to the data provided, out of the five students sampled, four (80%) have successfully paid their tuition fees in full, indicating a high level of financial commitment and responsibility among students.

A closer examination of the data reveals that 60% of students (UT001, UT002, and UT004) have utilized scholarships to offset their tuition costs, with an average scholarship amount of ₹3,500. This suggests that the institution's scholarship program is effective in supporting students from diverse backgrounds. Furthermore, 40% of students (UT003 and UT005) have opted for graduate programs, indicating a growing interest in advanced education among students.

The data also highlights the importance of timely payment of tuition fees, with 60% of students (UT001, UT002, and UT004) having paid their outstanding balances within the specified timeframe. This achievement is particularly noteworthy, given that the institution's revenue allocation methodology prioritizes student-credit-weighted formula for tuition allocation.

The sample data provides valuable insights into student billing patterns, underscoring the need for continued support and guidance in financial planning and management. As the institution continues to evolve and adapt to changing student needs, it is essential to monitor and analyze these trends to inform policy decisions and improve overall student outcomes.

Net Position Trend (2018-2022)

Visual representation of the institution's net financial position over the last five years, showing a shift from surplus to deficit.

Financial Narrative

The financial performance of the institution has been a subject of interest for stakeholders. A review of the data reveals that the net position has fluctuated significantly over the years. In the initial years, the net position was in surplus, with a peak of ₹1,580,000 recorded in 2018. However, this trend reversed in subsequent years, with the net position turning into a deficit by 2020. The deficit continued to widen, reaching ₹720,000 by 2022.

A closer examination of the data reveals that the operating activities have been a major contributor to the fluctuations in the net position. The net surplus/(deficit) from operating activities has ranged from ₹30,000 to ₹-720,000 over the years. Notably, the depreciation and amortization adjustments have consistently contributed ₹30,000 to the net surplus each year.

The data also highlights the impact of liabilities on the institution's financial performance. The total liabilities have increased steadily over the years, reaching a high of ₹1,580,000 by 2022. This has put pressure on the institution's ability to manage its finances effectively. Despite efforts to reduce liabilities, the institution has struggled to achieve a stable net position.
