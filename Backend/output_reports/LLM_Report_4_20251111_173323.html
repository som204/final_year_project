Overview of the Academic Year 2022-2023

The academic year demonstrated significant progress in student career outcomes, with placement data available for 18 students across five distinct departments. Graduates secured competitive compensation packages, ranging from 5.2 LPA to an impressive 28.8 LPA, reflecting the strong industry demand for our talent. These placements were facilitated by a diverse array of leading national and multinational corporations, underscoring the breadth of opportunities available to our students.

Key achievements include the highest CTC of 28.8 LPA secured by Amelia Davis from the IT department with Capgemini, closely followed by Harper Miller, also from IT, who secured 26.3 LPA with IBM. The IT department consistently attracted high-value placements, with other notable offers including 24.9 LPA with Accenture and 16.2 LPA with Capgemini. The CSE department also performed strongly, securing placements with companies like Amazon (21.3 LPA) and Google (21.4 LPA).

Student success was evident across various academic performances, with high achievers like Noah Miller from IT, boasting a 9.7 CGPA, securing 10.2 LPA with IBM, and Ethan Gonzalez from Chemical Engineering, with a 9.42 CGPA, securing 8.1 LPA with ONGC. The placements spanned a wide range of industries, including technology giants such as Amazon, Google, IBM, and Wipro; energy sector leaders like Reliance, ONGC, and Shell; and manufacturing firms such as ABB and Samsung, showcasing the versatility and industry readiness of our graduates.

Overview of Student Enrollment and CGPA

The institution currently enrolls a total of 6500 students across its departments. The Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department holds the largest student body with 2200 students, followed by Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) with 1600 students. The Information Technology (IT) department has an enrollment of 1200 students, while the Chemical Engineering department accounts for 800 students. The Automobile Engineering and Industrial Engineering (AEIE) department has the smallest enrollment with 700 students.

An analysis of student academic performance, based on a sample of 20 students, indicates a diverse range of Cumulative Grade Point Averages (CGPA). The highest recorded CGPA in this sample is 9.7, achieved by a student from the IT department, while the lowest CGPA observed is 6.08, from a student in the CSE department. The overall average CGPA for this sample stands at approximately 7.95, suggesting a generally satisfactory academic standing among the sampled students.

Departmental performance, as reflected in the sampled CGPA data, shows variations. The IT department demonstrates strong academic outcomes within the sample, with an an average CGPA of 8.85. The Chemical Engineering department also shows a commendable average CGPA of 7.80. In contrast, the CSE department's sampled students achieved an average CGPA of 7.29, and the ECE department's sampled students averaged 7.37. The AEIE department's single sampled student recorded a CGPA of 6.19. Supporting the academic environment, the Chemical Engineering department maintains the most favorable student-to-faculty ratio at 32.00 students per faculty member, followed by AEIE at 35.00. The IT department has a ratio of 42.86, ECE at 44.44, and CSE has the highest ratio at 52.38 students per faculty member.

It is important to note that a comprehensive assessment of academic performance, including pass rates, could not be fully conducted due to the absence of specific pass rate data. The CGPA analysis presented is based on a limited sample of 20 students and may not fully represent the entire student population's academic standing across all departments.

Financial Performance Summary

The institution's financial performance for the period reflects a robust operating position, with total operating revenue reaching ₹1,015,000. This was primarily driven by Tuition & Fees contributing ₹425,000, followed by Government Grants & Contracts at ₹280,000, and Research Grants & Sponsored Programs adding ₹150,000. Total operating expenses amounted to ₹1,000,000, with Salaries & Benefits representing the largest expenditure at ₹540,000. Academic Program Expenses stood at ₹120,000, and Research Expenses at ₹110,000, indicating significant investment in core academic and research activities.

The institution successfully achieved an operating surplus of ₹15,000, aligning precisely with the planned operating surplus of ₹15,000. Actual total revenue of ₹1,015,000 exceeded the budgeted total revenue of ₹1,000,000, demonstrating effective revenue generation. However, actual total expenses of ₹1,000,000 were slightly higher than the budgeted total expenses of ₹985,000. After accounting for non-operating items, including net investment gains of ₹10,000 and interest expense of -₹3,000, the net surplus before transfers was ₹22,000. Including transfers and capital grants of ₹8,000, the change in net position for the period concluded at ₹30,000.

An analysis of departmental financial health reveals positive contributions across all units. The Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department generated the highest surplus at ₹30,000, with revenues of ₹150,000 against expenses of ₹120,000. The Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) department followed with a surplus of ₹18,000 from revenues of ₹110,000 and expenses of ₹92,000. The Information Technology (IT) department recorded a surplus of ₹15,000, while the Applied Electronics and Instrumentation Engineering (AEIE) department achieved a surplus of ₹8,000. The Chemical Engineering department also contributed positively with a surplus of ₹5,000.

Overview of Placements and Career Services

The academic year demonstrated significant progress in student placements, with 19 students successfully securing positions across various industries. The average Cost to Company (CTC) achieved by these students stood at 15.7 LPA, reflecting competitive compensation packages.

Leading companies actively participated in the recruitment drive, with Amazon, ONGC, IBM, Capgemini, Accenture, and Texas Instruments each extending multiple offers. Other prominent recruiters included Reliance, TCS, Wipro, ABB, Google, Shell, and Samsung. These opportunities spanned diverse departments such as Chemical Engineering, Computer Science Engineering, Information Technology, Applied Electronics & Instrumentation Engineering, and Electronics & Communication Engineering. Individual CTCs ranged from 5.2 LPA to a high of 28.8 LPA, showcasing a wide spectrum of roles and compensation levels secured by the graduating cohort.

The institution's career services played a pivotal role in facilitating these placements, effectively bridging the gap between student talent and industry requirements. The consistent engagement with leading corporations ensured that students were presented with quality career opportunities, enabling them to embark on professional journeys with reputable organizations.
