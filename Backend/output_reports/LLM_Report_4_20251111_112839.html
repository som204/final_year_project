Academic & Research Overview

Section: Academics

Student & Faculty Distribution

Visual breakdown of student enrollment and faculty qualifications across key departments.

Academic Performance

The student body is distributed across five key departments, with the Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department having the largest enrollment at 2200 students. This is followed by the Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) department with 1600 students, the Information Technology (IT) department with 1200 students, the Chemical Engineering (CHEMICAL) department with 800 students, and the Aerospace Engineering and Applied Electronics (AEIE) department with 700 students. The student-faculty ratio varies across departments, with Chemical Engineering at 32:1 and CSE at 52:1, reflecting different resource allocations.

Research & Publications

The institution demonstrated a strong commitment to advancing research, successfully securing ₹150,000 in external funding from research grants and sponsored programs. This significant inflow of external capital underscores the quality and relevance of the research initiatives undertaken by our faculty and scholars. Complementing this, the university allocated substantial internal resources, with ₹110,000 dedicated to research expenses, directly supporting the operational needs of various projects and scholarly activities. This internal financial commitment ensures that our research infrastructure remains robust and that faculty have the necessary resources to pursue pioneering work.

Financial Narrative

The institution's financial plan for the period reflects a stable and forward-looking strategy, with a total budgeted revenue of ₹1,000,000 against total budgeted expenses of ₹985,000, resulting in a planned operating surplus of ₹15,000. An analysis of departmental performance reveals positive financial outcomes across all academic units, which collectively generated a total surplus of ₹76,000. The Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department was the largest contributor, achieving a surplus of ₹30,000.

Section: Placements

Placements & Career Services

The academic year demonstrated significant progress in student placements, marked by the active participation of a diverse portfolio of leading multinational corporations and core engineering firms. Esteemed organisations such as Amazon, Google, Microsoft, IBM, Capgemini, and TCS were prominent recruiters. The highest Cost to Company (CTC) offered was 28.8 LPA, secured by a student from the Information Technology department. Across all departments, the placements recorded an impressive average CTC of 13.58 LPA and a median CTC of 12.0 LPA. The relationship between CGPA and placement success indicates a nuanced evaluation by recruiters, with a student holding a 6.08 CGPA securing a package of 21.4 LPA from Google, highlighting a holistic assessment approach.

Student & Faculty Accomplishments

The academic year was marked by outstanding scholastic achievements. Benjamin Wilson from the CSE department secured a remarkable CGPA of 9.9, and Noah Miller from the IT department achieved a 9.7 CGPA. The institution is supported by a robust academic team of 151 faculty members, with the CSE department having the largest contingent of 42. An analysis of faculty qualifications indicates a strong commitment to advanced academic credentials, with a high concentration of Ph.D., M.Tech, and M.Sc degrees, ensuring a high standard of academic delivery.
