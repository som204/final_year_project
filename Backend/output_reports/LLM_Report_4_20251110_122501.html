Summary of the Year

The 2022-2023 academic year concluded with a demonstration of robust financial health and operational efficiency. The institution generated a Total Operating Revenue of ₹1,015,000, successfully managing its Total Operating Expenses at ₹1,000,000. This prudent fiscal management resulted in a positive operating surplus of ₹15,000. After accounting for non-operating items and capital grants, the institution recorded a final Change in Net Position of ₹30,000, underscoring a year of sustainable growth and stability.

The institution's revenue streams remained diversified, with Tuition and Fees serving as the primary contributor at ₹425,000. Significant financial support was also derived from Government Grants & Contracts, amounting to ₹280,000, and Research Grants & Sponsored Programs, which provided ₹150,000. On the expenditure side, the largest investment was directed towards human capital, with Salaries & Benefits constituting ₹540,000 of the total expenses. This commitment to personnel was complemented by substantial allocations to core mission activities, including ₹120,000 for Academic Program Expenses and ₹110,000 for Research Expenses.

This strong financial foundation directly supports the institution's commitment to academic excellence and student success. The allocation of ₹120,000 towards Academic Program Expenses and a further ₹70,000 for Student Services & Scholarships highlights a direct investment in enhancing the educational environment and supporting student outcomes. The operational surplus achieved this year positions the institution favourably to continue strengthening its academic offerings, research initiatives, and placement support services in the forthcoming year, ensuring a continued focus on high-quality education and career development for its students.

Performance Analysis

The institution's student body comprises a total of 6,500 individuals distributed across five key engineering departments. The Department of Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) maintains the largest enrollment with 2,200 students, followed by Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) with 1,600 students and Information Technology (IT) with 1,200 students. The Chemical Engineering and Applied Electronics & Instrumentation Engineering (AEIE) departments have more specialised student populations, with enrollments of 800 and 700 students, respectively.

An analysis of academic performance, based on a sample of first-year students from the CSE department, reveals a wide distribution of scholastic achievement. The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) within this cohort ranged from a high of 9.9 to a low of 5.09. A notable segment of students demonstrated exemplary performance, with five out of 28 students achieving a CGPA of 9.0 or higher. The majority of the students performed in the satisfactory to good range, with fourteen students securing a CGPA between 6.0 and 7.99. A smaller group of five students recorded a CGPA below 6.0, identifying a cohort that may benefit from targeted academic support initiatives.

Financial Analysis

The institution demonstrated a stable financial position, concluding the period with a positive change in net position of ₹30,000. Total operating revenues reached ₹1,015,000 against total operating expenses of ₹1,000,000, resulting in an operating surplus of ₹15,000. The primary revenue streams were Tuition & Fees, contributing ₹425,000, followed by Government Grants & Contracts at ₹280,000 and Research Grants & Sponsored Programs at ₹150,000. On the expenditure side, Salaries & Benefits constituted the largest component at ₹540,000, with other significant outlays including Academic Program Expenses (₹120,000) and Research Expenses (₹110,000).

An analysis of departmental finances reveals that all academic units operated with a surplus. The Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department was the most significant contributor, generating revenues of ₹150,000 against expenses of ₹120,000, yielding a surplus of ₹30,000. The Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) department also performed strongly with a surplus of ₹18,000 from revenues of ₹110,000. Other departments, including Information Technology (₹15,000 surplus), AEIE (₹8,000 surplus), and Chemical Engineering (₹5,000 surplus), all maintained positive financial outcomes, underscoring their operational efficiency.

Placement Analysis

The academic year demonstrated significant progress in graduate placements, reflecting a robust industry engagement and the high calibre of our students. The placement drive culminated in a highest salary package of 28.8 LPA, secured by a student from the Information Technology department with Capgemini. Students secured coveted roles in various multinational corporations, underscoring the institution's success in preparing industry-ready professionals.

A detailed departmental analysis reveals exceptional performance, particularly in the technology-focused streams. Research Funding and Strategy

The institution's research and development activities were significantly bolstered by external funding during the academic year. A total of ₹150,000 was secured through Research Grants and Sponsored Programs from various funding agencies. This was further augmented by substantial support from Government Grants and Contracts, which contributed ₹280,000 towards the institution's academic and research objectives, underscoring a strong engagement with national-level initiatives and sponsored projects.

In addition to external funding, the institution remains committed to fostering a robust research culture through dedicated internal support. A total of ₹110,000 was expended on direct research activities, covering costs associated with labs and program execution. The allocation of internal research funds to departments is a key component of this strategy. Furthermore, the institution employs a structured methodology for distributing research overhead, which is allocated to departments based on their direct grant receipts, thereby incentivizing the pursuit of externally sponsored projects.

The distribution of these financial resources facilitates research across various academic departments. While specific departmental focus areas are determined by the nature of the secured grants, the allocation mechanism ensures that both internal funds and overhead recovery from external grants are channelled to support departmental-level research priorities. This system directly links funding allocation to successful grant acquisition, promoting a competitive and productive research environment throughout the institution.

Faculty Profile

Distribution of faculty qualifications and departmental student-faculty ratios.

Faculty Analysis

The institution's academic staff comprises 151 faculty members distributed across five key departments. The Department of Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) is the largest, with 42 faculty members, followed by Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) with 36, Information Technology (IT) with 28, Chemical Engineering with 25, and Applied Electronics and Instrumentation Engineering (AEIE) with 20. An analysis of a representative sample of faculty reveals a diverse qualification profile, including Ph.D., M.Tech, M.Sc., and B.Tech degrees. Within this sample, approximately 21% of the CSE faculty hold a doctorate, while the ECE and Chemical departments have 17% and 12.5% Ph.D. holders, respectively, underscoring a strong foundation of advanced academic credentials across disciplines.

An analysis of the faculty-to-student ratio highlights varying levels of academic engagement across departments. The Chemical Engineering and AEIE departments maintain the most favorable ratios at approximately 1:32 and 1:35, respectively, allowing for more focused student-faculty interaction. The IT and ECE departments operate with ratios of approximately 1:43 and 1:44. The CSE department, with the largest student body of 2200, has the highest ratio at approximately 1:52. These metrics provide a clear quantitative framework for strategic academic planning and resource allocation to uphold educational standards across all programs.

Strategic Outlook

Building upon the solid financial foundation established in the 2022-2023 fiscal year, which concluded with a positive change in net position of ₹30,000, our primary strategic initiative for the upcoming year is to enhance our core operational strengths. We will focus on sustaining and growing our primary revenue streams, particularly the ₹150,000 generated from Research Grants & Sponsored Programs and the ₹280,000 from Government Grants & Contracts. Concurrently, we will maintain prudent fiscal management, ensuring that our largest expenditure, Salaries & Benefits at ₹540,000, continues to support the recruitment and retention of high-calibre faculty and staff, which is essential for delivering quality education and research.

Our vision for academic and research enhancement is directly linked to strategic resource allocation. In the past year, we dedicated ₹120,000 to Academic Program Expenses and ₹110,000 to Research Expenses. Moving forward, our goal is to leverage these investments to foster innovation and expand our scholarly output. A key objective is to increase funding for sponsored research, aiming to surpass the current income of ₹150,000 from these grants. This will be complemented by our continued commitment to student success, building upon the ₹70,000 allocated to Student Services & Scholarships to ensure a supportive and enriching learning environment.

Looking towards long-term development, our financial strategy is centered on sustainable growth and infrastructure renewal. The operating surplus of ₹15,000, augmented by net investment gains of ₹10,000, provides a base for future capital projects. We plan to strategically allocate future surpluses to enhance our physical campus, guided by our annual investment of ₹90,000 in Facilities, Maintenance & Utilities. Furthermore, a long-term priority is to expand our philanthropic support systems to grow our endowment, building on the ₹90,000 in donations from the previous year to secure the institution's financial future and support its mission for generations to come.
