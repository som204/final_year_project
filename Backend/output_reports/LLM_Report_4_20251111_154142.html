Executive Summary

A high-level overview of the academic year
2022-2023

Summary of Performance

The academic year demonstrated significant financial stability and operational efficiency. The institution concluded the period with a total operating revenue of ₹1,015,000 against total operating expenses of ₹1,000,000, resulting in a healthy operating surplus of ₹15,000. After accounting for net investment gains and other non-operating items, the final change in net position was a positive ₹30,000. This robust financial health was primarily driven by revenues from Tuition & Fees (₹425,000) and Government Grants & Contracts (₹280,000), while prudent management of expenditures was maintained, with Salaries & Benefits representing the largest component at ₹540,000.

On the academic front, departmental performance underscored the institution's strengths, particularly in technology and engineering disciplines. The Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) department was a standout contributor, generating the highest departmental surplus of ₹30,000 while serving the largest student body of 2,200. Other key departments, including Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) and Information Technology (IT), also demonstrated strong financial viability with surpluses of ₹18,000 and ₹15,000, respectively. Collectively, these departments highlight a core academic structure that is both popular among students and operationally sound.

The institution's positive financial outcome, characterized by a ₹15,000 operating surplus, provides a solid foundation for future strategic initiatives. The demonstrated strength and financial self-sufficiency of high-demand departments such as CSE, ECE, and IT position the institution to further invest in these core areas. This financial stability will be crucial in pursuing continued academic excellence, enhancing research capabilities funded by the ₹150,000 in existing grants, and expanding student support services in the upcoming year. The focus will remain on leveraging these operational successes to reinforce our standing as a leading educational institution.

Fiscal Performance Narrative

The institution's financial performance for the period reflects a stable and well-managed fiscal position. Total operating revenue reached ₹1,015,000 against total operating expenses of ₹1,000,000, resulting in an operating surplus of ₹15,000. This outcome successfully met the planned operating surplus target for the year. After accounting for non-operating items, including net investment gains of ₹10,000, and transfers, the institution recorded a final change in net position of ₹30,000, indicating positive financial growth and effective resource management.

A diversified revenue stream underpinned the institution's financial health. Tuition and fees remained the primary source of income, contributing ₹425,000 to the total revenue. This was substantially supported by external funding, with Government Grants and Contracts providing ₹280,000 and Research Grants and Sponsored Programs adding another ₹150,000. Further contributions from Donations and Endowment Income (₹90,000) and Auxiliary Enterprises (₹45,000) demonstrate a robust and multi-faceted approach to revenue generation.

Placements & Career Services

2022-2023

Placement Data Visualization

Distribution of student performance, which correlates with placement success.

Recruitment Cycle Summary

The recent placement cycle witnessed robust participation from a diverse range of leading corporations, resulting in successful career outcomes for students across multiple engineering disciplines. Prominent recruiters included IBM, Capgemini, Accenture, Amazon, ONGC, and Texas Instruments, each extending multiple offers to the graduating cohort. Other notable companies such as Google, TCS, Wipro, Shell, and Reliance also made significant contributions to the recruitment drive, reinforcing the institution's strong industry connections.

An analysis of department-wise placements indicates strong performance, particularly from the Information Technology department, which secured 7 offers from firms like Capgemini, IBM, and Accenture. The Computer Science and Engineering and Chemical Engineering departments followed with 4 placements each, with students receiving offers from top-tier companies including Amazon, Google, ONGC, and Shell. The Electronics and Communication Engineering department saw 3 students placed with industry leaders like Texas Instruments and Samsung, while the AEIE department secured a placement with ABB.

The compensation packages offered to students reflect a highly positive trend. The highest Cost to Company (CTC) package secured was 28.8 LPA, offered by Capgemini to a student from the IT department. The average CTC for the cohort stood at approximately 15.32 LPA, with a median CTC of 13.0 LPA, indicating a competitive and well-distributed salary range. The diversity in offers, from 5.2 LPA to 28.8 LPA, highlights the breadth of opportunities available to students, catering to various roles and industry sectors.

Academic Performance

Enrollment Visualization

A visual representation of student distribution across key departments.

Student Body and Achievement

The institution's student body comprises 6,500 students distributed across five key departments. The Department of Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) has the largest enrollment with 2,200 students, followed by Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) with 1,600 students, and Information Technology (IT) with 1,200 students. The Chemical Engineering and Applied Electronics and Instrumentation Engineering (AEIE) departments have smaller student populations of 800 and 700, respectively. The student population includes both undergraduate and graduate level students, indicating a diverse academic community pursuing a range of qualifications.

An analysis of departmental resources reveals varying student-faculty ratios across the disciplines. The Chemical Engineering department maintains the most favorable ratio at 32:1, with 25 faculty members for its 800 students. The AEIE department follows with a ratio of 35:1. The ratios in the larger departments are higher, with IT at approximately 43:1, ECE at 44:1, and the CSE department recording the highest student-faculty ratio at approximately 52:1, with 42 faculty members serving its 2,200 students.

Faculty Profile and Leadership

The institution's faculty profile is distinguished by a robust blend of academic qualifications and extensive professional experience. The experience spectrum is notably broad, ranging from 5 to 20 years, with several members such as Wendy Lee, Steven Lee, and Helen Clark possessing two decades of experience in their respective fields.

Departmental leadership is well-defined, with experienced academics like Michael Owen heading the CSE department as Head of Department. The faculty structure demonstrates a healthy balance between senior and junior positions, with a strategic mix of Professor and Assistant Professor roles across departments. This blend of seasoned professors and dynamic assistant professors fosters a rich environment for mentorship and academic innovation. The presence of highly experienced faculty, such as Uma Anderson with 17 years and Nina Davis with 18 years of experience, underscores the depth of expertise available to students at all levels of their academic journey.

Research Funding and Expenditure

The institution's commitment to fostering a robust research ecosystem was underscored by significant financial inflows from external agencies. This expenditure, well-aligned with the internal allocation, facilitated a wide range of scholarly pursuits, from laboratory work to fieldwork and data analysis. Future Goals & Vision

Strategic direction for the next 3-5 years
2022-2023

Strategic Vision

Building upon the current academic profile of our first-year Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) cohort, our strategic vision for the next 3-5 years is centered on elevating student achievement and fostering a culture of excellence. The performance of the current intake, which shows an average CGPA of approximately 7.4, provides a crucial baseline for our future academic objectives. Our primary goal is to enhance this academic standing by implementing targeted strategies aimed at both supporting students who are facing challenges and providing advanced opportunities for high-achievers. This will involve a focused effort to improve overall cohort performance and ensure every student is positioned for success.

To achieve these academic goals, we will launch key initiatives designed to enhance the student experience and outcomes. Analysis of the current CSE cohort indicates that approximately 18% of students have a CGPA below 6.0, while a similar percentage maintains a CGPA above 9.0. In response, our plan includes the development of a structured academic support program for students requiring assistance, alongside an enrichment track offering early research and project-based learning for top performers. Future financial planning will prioritize investment in these areas, allocating resources towards mentorship programs, specialized workshops, and faculty development to support this dual-pronged approach to student development.

Our long-term vision for research and innovation is intrinsically linked to nurturing our undergraduate talent. By engaging our high-achieving students in departmental research activities early in their academic careers, we aim to cultivate a robust pipeline of future innovators. This strategy is designed to increase undergraduate participation in faculty-led projects, which will strengthen our research ecosystem. The success of this initiative will be tracked through metrics related to student involvement in research and its eventual impact on departmental grant receipts, aligning with our institutional model of allocating research overhead based on direct grant funding. This focus will ensure sustainable growth in our research and innovation capacity.
